A real kid. A broken checkpoint. A three-tier plan.

  • Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma survivor · severe growth failure · nephrocalcinosis

The cause — confirmed

100.0 rank points / F-max 1.000 — full match, Track 1

The hard truth

  • No approved drug raises BubR1.
  • We cannot poison the checkpoint further — it is already broken.
  • So we map three attack surfaces: the tumor, the stress, the protein.

Mechanism: genotype to drug

Tier A — treat what we see today

Adavosertib + irinotecan — pediatric trial defines the dose (NCT02095132), rhabdomyosarcoma arm
Potassium citrate + thiazide — standard, safe, bone-protective for nephrocalcinosis
Alisertib — alternate mitotic driver (COG ADVL0921)

Tier B — buffer the stress

Aneuploid cells turn on mTOR and pour out inflammatory signals.
Everolimus quiets that axis — deepest pediatric safety record (EXIST-1, transplant).
Honest claim: lowers the fire, does not fix the checkpoint.

Tier C — restore the protein (innovation)

In mice, BubR1 overexpression repairs the checkpoint and extends lifespan (Baker 2013).
This child still makes weakened BubR1 from his missense allele.
Three routes: AAV augmentation · SIRT2-axis stabilization · peptide rescue

Rigor: three independent methods agree

Panel scan · Exomiser genome-wide (94 s, no panel) · clinical databases — all point to BUB1B
